# The equality-surface root index at real minimum two

For real \(2\le a\le b\), set \(c=b(b+2)-a\) and \(s=a+b+c\).
The ordered eigenvalues of the six-support complement quotient satisfy

\[
0=\lambda_1<\lambda_2<\lambda_3=b<a+b<s
<\lambda_4\le\lambda_5\le\lambda_6.
\]

In particular, \(b\) is simple. If also \(h_C(1)=0\), then
\(\lambda_2=1\) is simple, and dividing the quintic by
\((x-1)(x-b)\) leaves a monic cubic whose three roots all exceed
\(s\). This strengthens Proposition 14.6 of the supporting manuscript
from minimum four to minimum two. The general endpoint-one residual-root
window still has its separate minimum-four hypothesis. The selected
26-page classification does not use this supporting proposition.

Write \(S=W^{1/2}CW^{-1/2}\), where
\(W=\operatorname{diag}(a,b,c,ab,ac,bc)\). Its positive edge-energy
form and connected support graph show that it is positive semidefinite
with a one-dimensional zero kernel generated by a vector with every
coordinate nonzero. Hence each proper principal block is positive definite.
Ordering holds because \(c-b=b^2+(b-a)>0\), so the exceptional global-gap
point \((2,2,2)\) cannot occur on this surface.

Delete singleton support 2. The five-support principal block \(B\)
is the direct sum of the scalar \(b\) on support 13 and the block
\(T\) on supports 1, 3, 12, 23. Direct expansion gives

\[
\det(xI-T)=(x-b)K(x),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
K(x)={}&x^3-b(b^2+4b+5)x^2\\
&+[ac(b^2+1)+b^2(b+2)(b^2+3b+3)]x-abc(b+3).
\end{aligned}
\]

Its three roots are positive and real. At \(b\),

\[
K(b)=b(b+1)[a(b-a)(b-2)+ab(b-2)(b+1)+b^2(b+1)(b+2)]>0.
\]

The first two terms are nonnegative throughout the claimed domain;
the third is positive, including at \(a=b=2\). The pair-support
principal block of \(T\) is \(\operatorname{diag}(c,a)\), so its
largest eigenvalue is at least \(c>b\). The positive cubic value at
\(b\) permits either one or three roots below \(b\); the root above
\(b\) rules out three. Thus \(B\) has exactly one eigenvalue below
\(b\), two equal to \(b\), and two above. Principal interlacing
forces \(\lambda_3=b\).

Since \(K(b)\ne0\), the kernel of \(B-bI\) is two-dimensional.
The support-13 unit vector couples to the deleted singleton with
\(-\sqrt{abc}\ne0\). Projecting the full eigenvector equation onto
this vector forces the deleted coordinate to vanish. The remaining
vectors in \(\ker(B-bI)\) obey one nonzero linear coupling condition,
so their space is one-dimensional. This proves that \(b\) is simple.

The established global total-sum gap, valid for minimum two, gives
\(\lambda_4>s\). If one is a root, it is below \(b\ge2\) and
therefore must be \(\lambda_2\); its neighbors zero and \(b\) prove
simplicity. The remaining three roots exceed \(s\), yielding the cubic
claim without using the minimum-four residual-window theorem.

Run from the repository root with Python 3.10 or later and SymPy 1.14.0:

```sh
python3 scripts/verify_equality_spectrum.py
```

The checker reconstructs the principal and full determinants directly
from the set-disjointness quotient and verifies the identities, weighted
symmetry and coupling. Three specified controls \((2,2,6)\),
\((2,3,13)\) and \((3,3,12)\) have exact root counts: after removing
zero and \(b\), one root lies in \((0,b)\), none in \((b,s)\),
and three above \(s\). These controls do not satisfy endpoint one;
they check the unconditional index statement below the former threshold.
They supplement the written real-domain proof. Results are saved in
`results/equality-spectrum-minimum-two.json`.

The support model and SymPy backend are shared with earlier checks;
the original characteristic-polynomial helper is not imported. No
parameter scan, integer-point enumeration, historical finite-base rerun,
expanded graph calculation, floating-point spectrum or Lean validation
is used. The equality-curve integer-point problem remains open.
