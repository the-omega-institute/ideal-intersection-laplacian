# Two infinite families with nonintegral Laplacian spectrum

Let $G_n$ have the nonzero proper ideals of $\mathbb Z_n$ as vertices,
with distinct ideals adjacent when their intersection is nonzero. Its
Laplacian is $L=D-A$. We prove:

1. If $n$ is squarefree with $t\geq3$ distinct prime factors, $G_n$ is not
   Laplacian integral.
2. If $n=pq r^k$, where $p,q,r$ are distinct primes and $k\geq1$, $G_n$ is
   not Laplacian integral.

The first result uses a two- or three-dimensional invariant subspace for
every $t$; it does not require the complete spectrum. The second supplies
an explicit quadratic obstruction for an unequal-exponent family.

These are written follow-on deductions for the Q3 collaboration proposed by
Reza Nikandish. The source preprint remains confidential and is not reproduced
here. Coauthor review and comparison with the wider literature remain pending.
The general characterization for arbitrary exponent vectors is open.

## The graph convention

Write $n=\prod_i p_i^{k_i}$. A divisor $d=\prod_i p_i^{a_i}$ identifies the
ideal $(d)$, where $0\leq a_i\leq k_i$. Exclude the all-zero exponent vector
(the whole ring) and $(k_i)_i$ (the zero ideal). Since the intersection of
two principal ideals is generated by the least common multiple, distinct
vertices $a,b$ are adjacent exactly when

$$\max(a_i,b_i)<k_i\quad\text{for at least one }i.$$

Set $S(a)=\{i:a_i<k_i\}$. This gives the equivalent condition
$S(a)\cap S(b)\ne\varnothing$. For squarefree $n$, the vertices thus
identify with the nonempty proper subsets of $[t]$.

The graph originates in Chakrabarty, Ghosh, Mukherjee and Sen,
[*Intersection graphs of ideals of rings*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2008.11.034),
Discrete Mathematics 309 (2009), 5381-5392. The coordinate description used
below follows directly from ideal intersection in $\mathbb Z_n$.

## Squarefree integers: an elementary invariant-subspace proof

Use the two basis vectors $e_0,e_1$ to encode absence or presence of a
coordinate in a subset. On the space of all subsets set

$$E=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
F=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

The entry of $F^{\otimes t}$ at $(S,T)$ is $1$ exactly when $S\cap T$ is
empty. If $c=2^t-1$, the principal submatrix of

$$\Phi=cI-E^{\otimes t}+F^{\otimes t}-J^{\otimes t}$$

on the nonempty proper subsets is $L(G_n)$. Indeed, its diagonal entry is
$2^t-2-2^{t-|S|}$, the vertex degree, and its off-diagonal entry is $-1$
for intersecting subsets and $0$ otherwise.

For a pair of coordinates put

$$w=e_0\otimes e_1-e_1\otimes e_0.$$

For every two-by-two matrix $H$,
$H^{\otimes2}w=(\det H)w$. In particular, a factor $w$ contributes $2$
under $E$, $-1$ under $F$, and $0$ under $J$. Every tensor containing $w$
vanishes at the empty and full subsets: exactly one coordinate in that pair
is present. Consequently the subspaces below lie in the actual graph space;
the removal of the two endpoint subsets does not alter the restricted action.

### Odd number of prime factors

Let $t=2r+1$, $r\geq1$, and restrict to
$w^{\otimes r}\otimes\operatorname{span}\{e_0,e_1\}$.
Writing $a=2^r$ and $s=(-1)^r$, the restriction is

$$B=\begin{pmatrix}c-2a+s&s\\s&c-a\end{pmatrix}.$$

Its characteristic discriminant is

$$\Delta=(a-s)^2+4.$$

The integer $h=a-s$ is at least $3$: it equals $3$ for $r=1,2$ and is
larger thereafter. Thus

$$h^2<\Delta=h^2+4<(h+1)^2.$$

The discriminant is not a square, so both eigenvalues of $B$ are irrational.
As eigenvalues of the restriction they occur in $L(G_n)$.

### Even number of prime factors

Let $t=2r$, $r\geq2$. Use $r-1$ paired factors $w$ and the symmetric
subspace on the last two coordinates. Its orthonormal basis is

$$e_0e_0,\qquad (e_0e_1+e_1e_0)/\sqrt2,\qquad e_1e_1.$$

Put $a=2^{r-1}\geq2$ and $s=(-1)^{r-1}$. The restriction of $L$ is $cI-K$,
where the real symmetric matrix is

$$K=\begin{pmatrix}
4a-s&-s\sqrt2&-s\\
-s\sqrt2&2a-s&0\\
-s&0&a
\end{pmatrix}.$$

The leading principal minors of $K-(a-1)I$ are

$$3a+1-s,$$
$$ (3a+1-s)(a+1-s)-2,$$
$$ (3a-s)(a+1-s)-2.$$

They are positive for $a\geq2$ and $s\in\{-1,1\}$; for example,
$3a-s\geq5$ and $a+1-s\geq2$ give a last minor at least $8$.
Sylvester's criterion therefore gives $\lambda_{\min}(K)>a-1$.

The principal submatrix of $K-aI$ on its first and third coordinates is

$$\begin{pmatrix}3a-s&-s\\-s&0\end{pmatrix},$$

whose determinant is $-1$. It has a negative Rayleigh quotient, hence
$\lambda_{\min}(K)<a$. Combining the two bounds gives

$$c-a<c-\lambda_{\min}(K)<c-a+1.$$

This is an eigenvalue of $L(G_n)$ strictly between consecutive integers.
This proves nonintegrality for all even $t\geq4$, completing the squarefree
case for every $t\geq3$.

**Corollary.** For squarefree composite $n$, $G_n$ is Laplacian integral
if and only if $n$ has exactly two prime factors. In that case its two
vertices are the ideals $(p)$ and $(q)$, with zero intersection, so the
graph is edgeless and its Laplacian is zero. The disconnected case is
included in this integrality statement.

## The unequal-exponent family $pq r^k$

The supports are subsets of $\{1,2,3\}$. The classes $\{1\}$ and $\{2\}$
have size $1$; the classes $\{1,3\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ have size $k$.
There are $4k+2$ vertices in total.

Consider functions taking the constant values $x,-x$ on the first two
classes and $y,-y$ on the latter two, and zero on all other supports.
Swapping coordinates $1,2$ negates these functions. Direct neighbor counts
show that their span is invariant, with restricted Laplacian

$$B_k=\begin{pmatrix}2k&-k\\-1&4k+1\end{pmatrix}.$$

For completeness, a vertex with support $\{1\}$ has $2k$ neighbors,
giving $(Lf)_{\{1\}}=2kx-ky$. A vertex with support $\{1,3\}$ has degree
$4k$. Subtracting its $k-1$ same-class values and the $k$ opposite-class
values gives $(Lf)_{\{1,3\}}=-x+(4k+1)y$. The swapped classes give the
negatives, while each remaining support sees equal weights with opposite
values and contributes zero.

The characteristic polynomial and discriminant are

$$\det(zI-B_k)=z^2-(6k+1)z+(8k^2+k),$$
$$\Delta_k=(2k+2)^2-3.$$

For every $k\geq1$,

$$ (2k+1)^2<\Delta_k<(2k+2)^2.$$

Hence $G_{pq r^k}$ has the irrational eigenvalues

$$\frac{6k+1\pm\sqrt{(2k+2)^2-3}}2.$$

Permuting the prime factors gives the same conclusion for every exponent
vector that is a permutation of $(1,1,k)$.

## A limitation of the repeated-exponent approach

For exponents $(2,2,3)$, the analogous antisymmetric block is

$$\begin{pmatrix}21&-6\\-2&32\end{pmatrix},$$

with integer eigenvalues $20,33$. Thus the existence of two equal exponents
alone does not make this block an obstruction.

An exact direct computation on all $34$ vertices instead gives the
class-constant characteristic polynomial

$$z(z-34)(z-33)(z-20)(z^3-80z^2+2099z-18052).$$

The cubic is irreducible modulo $5$: its values at residues $0,1,2,3,4$
are respectively $3,3,4,2,3$. A cubic over a field with no root is
irreducible. Gauss's lemma therefore makes this cubic irreducible over
$\mathbb Q$, establishing nonintegrality for this particular exponent vector.
The [exact calculation](../scripts/check_remaining_case.py) also checks the
full characteristic polynomial against the class decomposition.

## Verification and remaining question

[The standard-library checker](../scripts/check_integrality_obstructions.py)
enumerates ideals as exponent tuples and tests adjacency directly using
coordinatewise maxima. It verifies $LU=UB$ in integer arithmetic for the
written embeddings, for squarefree $t=3,\ldots,8$ and $(1,1,k)$ with
$k=1,\ldots,8$. It also checks the consecutive-square bounds and the
principal-minor witnesses. The [record](../results/integrality-obstructions.json)
reports the exact finite ranges and source hash.

The infinite-family conclusions follow from the proofs above. The finite
checks validate their explicit embeddings on representative instances.
The $(2,2,3)$ characteristic-polynomial check uses SymPy 1.14.0; no numerical
eigenvalue approximation or Lean run is used.

The remaining classification includes unequal exponent triples outside
$(1,1,k)$ and higher-dimensional exponent vectors that are not squarefree.
These results do not establish nonintegrality for all integers with at least
three distinct prime factors.
