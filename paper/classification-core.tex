\begin{abstract}
We prove that the ideal intersection graph of
$\mathbb Z_{p^a q^b r^c}$ is Laplacian nonintegral for any three distinct
primes and every positive exponent triple. Vertices are the nonzero
proper ideals; adjacency means nonzero intersection.

A six-support complement quotient $C$ supplies the spectral obstruction.
Symmetry and exact factor indices settle repeated exponents. For fully
distinct exponents beyond the small-minimum cases, a positive quotient
root below three reduces a hypothetical integer spectrum to endpoint
one or endpoint two. Endpoint two supplies a further root in $(4,5)$
at large minima; endpoint one reduces to two small-gap brackets and
the largest-root interval
\[
 bc+a+b+c<\lambda_{\max}(C)<bc+a+b+c+1
\]
for real $2\leq a\leq b\leq c$, $b-a\geq3$ and $c\geq a^2-a$.
The proof is computer-assisted: unbounded spectral arguments are combined
with complete exact checks on finite domains of $325$ pairs,
$27\,562$ triples and $8\,658$ triples. The required coefficient identities
and certificate specifications are supplied. Verification uses integer
and rational arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The ideal intersection graph records the intersection structure of the
nonzero proper ideals of a ring~\cite{intersection}. For $\mathbb Z_n$,
its isomorphism type depends on the prime exponents. We ask when its
combinatorial Laplacian has only integer eigenvalues.
Related ring graphs include Beck's coloring graph, zero-divisor graphs
and annihilating-ideal graphs~\cite{beck,anderson,behboodi1,behboodi2,aalipour}.
Their multiplication adjacency differs from the intersection convention
used here. Laplacian and graph-spectral background is developed
in~\cite{fiedler,grone,merris,cvetkovic,brouwer}; equitable partitions
and generalized joins reduce these graphs to small matrices~\cite{godsilroyle,cardoso}.

Nikandish~\cite[Theorems 13 and 30]{nikandish} gives a generalized-join spectrum formula
and a Schur--Weyl reduction for equal prime exponents, with representation
background in~\cite{fultonharris}. His nonintegrality result for $(pqr)^k$,
$k\geq1$~\cite[Corollary 31]{nikandish},
is extended here to every positive three-prime exponent triple
(Theorem~\ref{thm:three-prime-classification}).
The support-class graph model makes the prime values irrelevant.
The main difficulty is therefore the unbounded exponent domain,
including fully unequal exponents whose complement quotient can have
integer low roots.

Our main result is that every graph in this three-prime family is
Laplacian nonintegral, for all positive integers $a,b,c$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:three-prime-classification}). The multiplicatively related
support weights permit symmetry reduction, exact factor arithmetic and
real-parameter spectral separation across the unbounded exponent domain.

The proof separates three cases. Theorem~\ref{thm:repeated} settles
repeated entries, and Corollary~\ref{cor:minimum-seven} settles minima at
most seven using endpoint signs, the uniform cutoff and complete finite
certificates. For $8\leq a<b<c$, Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-low-root}
puts a positive quotient root in $(0,3)$. Integrality forces it to be one
or two. Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-two-completion} excludes two. At one,
Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-one-small-gaps} handles middle gaps one and two;
for larger gaps, Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-one-spectrum} gives the
exponent-sum bound needed by Theorem~\ref{thm:largest-root-unit-interval}.
This completes the classification without enumerating integer points on
the endpoint-one surface.

The computer-assisted dependencies are stated where used and collected
in Appendix~\ref{sec:verification}. The complete coefficient tables
in the other appendices support the unbounded real arguments.
The higher-prime nonsquarefree classification remains open.
Further arithmetic obstructions, local equality-curve analysis and
higher-prime families are retained in the accompanying research
manuscript and repository~\cite{repository}. Local Diophantine
questions on that curve are separate from the spectral classification;
their general background is in~\cite{hindrysilverman,serre}.

\section{Further questions}
The three-prime classification is complete. The general nonsquarefree
problem with four or more distinct prime factors remains open outside
the established families in the supporting package. One structural
question is whether a quotient restriction or variational argument can
handle fully distinct higher-prime exponents without a separate expansion
for each repeated value. Effective integer-point enumeration on the
endpoint-one surface is another arithmetic question, independent of the
nonintegrality theorem proved here.

\section{Exact evidence for the classification}
\label{sec:verification}
The global theorem is computer-assisted. Its real-tail and small-gap
steps have written unbounded proofs; the finite bases below exhaust the
domains derived by those proofs. The source repository~\cite{repository}
retains the checker sources, certificates and reproduction instructions.
Python~3.10 or later and SymPy~1.14.0 use integer and rational arithmetic.
No Lean formalization is claimed for these Laplacian results.
The revision-pinned manifest in
\path{notes/classification-certificate-manifest.md} and
\path{results/classification-certificate-manifest.json} maps each essential
input to its source and data revisions, SHA256 hashes, checking command
and expected result. Historical scope strings in the saved reports describe
the state when those reports were produced; the dependencies below give
their role in the completed classification.

\subsection{Repeated entries and small minima}
The exact divisor reduction exhausts the first three nonboundary
factor indices by all divisors of $24,108,320$. The eight positive-$b$
cubics have complete root-free modular certificates; later indices use
the written cubic interval. The saved data are in
\path{results/repeated-exponent-completion.json} and the earlier
factor-index certificates. At minimum three, the proved cutoff leaves
exactly $325$ pairs $4\leq b<c<30$. The first endpoint has $257$ positive
and $68$ negative values with no zero. The complete polynomial and
separate integer-Horner certificate is in \path{results/distinct-tail.json}.

At minima four through seven, the complete domain
$a<b<c<4a^2-2a$ has $27\,562$ triples. Each row in
\path{results/open-region-discriminants.csv} records the integer
discriminant and consecutive-square brackets. Independent fraction-free
$9\times9$ Sylvester determinants, exact divisions, completeness and
absence of duplicate triples are checked by
\path{scripts/check_open_region_certificate.py}; its saved report is
\path{results/open-region-certificate-check.json}.
These finite inputs remain part of Corollary~\ref{cor:minimum-seven}.

\subsection{Endpoint-two completion}
Appendix~\ref{sec:endpoint-two-identities} displays the complete
$84$-, $44$- and $112$-term positive identities, the two nonnegative
square expressions and every per-minimum count.
\path{scripts/verify_endpoint_two_middle_cutoff.py} checks the cubic identity,
the $36$ positive coefficients at $a=3+m$ and the sharp boundary.
The necessary base at minima eight through thirty-nine contains exactly
$8\,658$ fully distinct triples. Integer Horner and $6\times6$ Bareiss
evaluations agree and give no endpoint zeros; the
\href{https://github.com/the-omega-institute/ideal-intersection-laplacian/blob/4afe151e1dc7193e6776cc1b08d3606aa804238e/scripts/check_endpoint_two_completion.py}{revision-pinned checker}
and its complete CSV and JSON preserve this evidence.
\path{scripts/verify_endpoint_two_coefficients.py} checks all $240$
displayed coefficients and $32$ domain counts by direct determinant
and an arithmetic formula.

\subsection{Written endpoint-one and largest-root steps}
The sum comparison uses the displayed quadratic with three positive coefficients.
Its residual-root separation has the sharp real cutoff $a>2+\sqrt3$;
\path{scripts/verify_residual_spectrum_cutoff.py} checks the anchor and sharpness identities.
The largest-root interval uses the positive $36$- and $170$-term identities
for $a\geq3$, a $35$-coefficient positive lower bound for $2\leq a\leq3$,
and a symmetric Schur complement. The two small-gap proofs use six
complete sign vectors at $a=2+m$, proving the brackets for all real $a\geq2$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:largest-root-identities}
retains every vector. The
\href{https://github.com/the-omega-institute/ideal-intersection-laplacian/blob/4471a5b4a02d97d3dcf78ea004298d5592924a95/scripts/check_largest_root_unit_interval.py}{revision-pinned largest-root checker}
reconstructs the generic quotient and independent Schur determinant,
the lower sign identity, every coefficient row and the historical small-gap
vectors at $a=8+m$.
Its fixed controls supplement the written proofs. The separate
checker \path{scripts/verify_largest_root_coefficients.py} reconstructs
the direct determinant and checks the original tables and current small-gap vectors against it.
The slab bound is checked by \path{scripts/verify_largest_root_minimum_two.py}.
The separate
minimum-eight and middle-at-most-fifteen certificates are unnecessary
for this route. Reorganizing the presentation does not rerun the
unchanged historical certificates or remove their mathematical role.
