\section{Small exponents and complement Rayleigh bounds}
\label{sec:unit}

The complement quotient replaces coordinate symmetry when the other
two exponents are arbitrary. A root between zero and one then settles the
entire unit-exponent family after the small exceptions are identified.
For arbitrary prime-factor count, a rational trial function also gives
an explicit sufficient condition with a distinguished exponent $m\geq2$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:unit}
For distinct primes $p,q,r$ and every $b,c\geq1$, the graph
$G_{p q^b r^c}$ is not Laplacian integral. The same holds for
permutations of the exponent vector $(1,b,c)$.
\end{theorem}

The equal-exponent decomposition is replaced by a complement identity
on all six support classes. For arbitrary $a,b,c\geq1$, remove the
$u=abc-1$ universal vertices and denote the remaining graph by $H$.
In the order $1,2,3,12,13,23$, its class-size vector is
$w=(a,b,c,ab,ac,bc)^T$ and its vertex count is
$N=a+b+c+ab+ac+bc$. Disjoint supports are adjacent in the complement
$\overline H$, whose cell-constant Laplacian quotient is
\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}
b+c+bc&-b&-c&0&0&-bc\\
-a&a+c+ac&-c&0&-ac&0\\
-a&-b&a+b+ab&-ab&0&0\\
0&0&-c&c&0&0\\
0&-b&0&0&b&0\\
-a&0&0&0&0&a
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Multiplication on the left by $\operatorname{diag}(w)$ makes $C$
symmetric. Moreover $C\mathbf1=0$ and $w^TC=0$.
If $B$ is the quotient of $H$, the complement relation gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complement}
B+C=NI-\mathbf1w^T.
\end{equation}
Thus an eigenvector $Cv=\mu v$ with $\mu\ne0$ satisfies $w^Tv=0$
and $Bv=(N-\mu)v$. When $N-\mu\ne0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:join}
lifts it to an eigenvalue $N+u-\mu$ of $G_n$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:unit}]
Write $\det(xI-C)=x h_{a,b,c}(x)$. Direct determinant expansion yields
\begin{align*}
h_{a,b,c}(0)&=-abc(a+b+c)N,\\
h_{a,b,c}(a)&=-a^2b^2c^2(a^2+2a-b-c).
\end{align*}
At $a=1$, the first value is strictly negative and
\[
h_{1,b,c}(1)=b^2c^2(b+c-3).
\]
When $b+c>3$, a real root $\mu$ lies strictly between $0$ and $1$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:complement} and the universal-vertex lift produce a
Laplacian eigenvalue $V-\mu\in(V-1,V)$, where $V=N+u$ is the integer
vertex count. This eigenvalue is noninteger. When $b+c\leq3$, the
positive pairs are $(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)$; Theorem~\ref{thm:pqrk}
settles the corresponding permutations of $(1,1,k)$. Permuting the
prime factors preserves the graph's isomorphism type.
\end{proof}

The unit-exponent obstruction extends to any number of prime factors
by a variational argument on the complement graph.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:unit-any-prime-count}
Let $n=\prod_{i=1}^t p_i^{k_i}$ with $t\geq3$, distinct primes
$p_i$ and positive integer exponents $k_i$. If some $k_i=1$, then
$G_n$ has a Laplacian eigenvalue in $(V-1,V)$, where
$V=\prod_{i=1}^t(k_i+1)-2$. In particular, $G_n$ is not
Laplacian integral.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Permute the factors so that $k_t=1$, and put
\[
 A=\prod_{i<t}k_i,\qquad
 B=\prod_{i<t}(k_i+1)-1-A.
\]
The quantity $B$ is the sum of the weights of all nonempty proper
supports on the first $t-1$ coordinates. Since $t-1\geq2$, it is
positive. Remove the $A-1$ full-support universal vertices and let
$F=\overline H$. Then $N=|H|=A+2B+1$ and $V=N+A-1=2A+2B$.
The graph $F$ is connected: its singleton support classes form a
connected complete multipartite graph, and every proper support has
a singleton outside it.

The support $\{t\}$ consists of a single vertex $z$. Each of the
$A$ vertices supported on $[t-1]$ has $z$ as its sole neighbor in
$F$. The remaining $2B$ vertices form two collections of weight $B$:
nonempty proper supports $S\subset[t-1]$, and their counterparts
$S\cup\{t\}$. The vertex $z$ is adjacent to all vertices of the
first collection and none of the second.

Give a function $f$ the value $2B$ on the $A$ leaves, zero at $z$,
and $-A$ on the other $2B$ vertices. It is nonzero and
orthogonal to the constant vector. Its Laplacian energy and norm are
\[
 f^TL(F)f=A(2B)^2+BA^2=AB(4B+A),\qquad
 f^Tf=A(2B)^2+2BA^2=2AB(2B+A).
\]
Indeed, only edges incident to $z$ have unequal endpoint values.
Connectedness and the variational characterization therefore give
\[
 0<\lambda_2(F)\leq\frac{4B+A}{4B+2A}<1.
\]
For a corresponding eigenvector, the complement identity
$L(H)+L(F)=NI-J$ gives the nonzero eigenvalue $N-\lambda_2(F)$
of $H$. Lemma~\ref{lem:join} lifts it to
$V-\lambda_2(F)\in(V-1,V)$, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

The restriction $t\geq3$ is sharp for this conclusion. If $t=2$
and $k_2=1$, then $B=0$ and $F=K_{1,A}$, so the complement and
universal-vertex identities give an integral spectrum for $G_n$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:unit-any-prime-count} also gives a proof of
Theorems~\ref{thm:squarefree} for $t\geq3$ and~\ref{thm:unit}
without their separate spectral decompositions or small exceptions.

The same support partition gives an obstruction when the distinguished
exponent is larger than one and the other support weights are sufficiently
concentrated on their full support.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:small-exponent-rayleigh}
Let $n=(\prod_{i<t}p_i^{k_i})p_t^m$ have $t\geq3$ distinct prime
factors and positive integer exponents. Put
\[
 A=\prod_{i<t}k_i,\qquad B=\prod_{i<t}(k_i+1)-1-A.
\]
If $A\geq(m^2-1)B-(m-1)$, then $G_n$ has a Laplacian eigenvalue
in $(V-1,V)$, where $V=(m+1)\prod_{i<t}(k_i+1)-2$.
In particular, it is not Laplacian integral. The condition includes
equality and can be applied after any permutation of the factors.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Remove the $mA-1$ universal vertices and let $F=\overline H$.
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:unit-any-prime-count}, $F$ is connected and
$B>0$. Its vertices split into three collections: $L$ supported on
$[t-1]$, $Z$ supported on $\{t\}$, and the remaining collection $R$.
Their sizes are $A,m,(m+1)B$, respectively. There are $mA$ edges
between $L$ and $Z$, none between $L$ and $R$, and $mB$ between
$Z$ and $R$. Among the vertices of $R$, those whose support contains
$t$ have total weight $mB$ and no neighbor in $Z$; the others have
total weight $B$ and are adjacent to every vertex of $Z$.

Give a trial function $f$ the values
\[
 x=m\ \text{on }L,\qquad y=m-1\ \text{on }Z,\qquad
 z=-\frac{m(A+m-1)}{(m+1)B}\ \text{on }R.
\]
It is nonzero and $Ax+my+(m+1)Bz=0$. Its energy $E$ and squared
norm $D$ are
\[
 E=mA(x-y)^2+mB(y-z)^2,\qquad
 D=Ax^2+my^2+(m+1)Bz^2.
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
 E-D=\frac{m\bigl((m^2-1)B-A-(m-1)\bigr)
                 \bigl(mA+(m^2-1)B+m(m-1)\bigr)}{B(m+1)^2}.
\]
The second factor is positive. Thus $E/D<1$ when the hypothesis
is strict, and the variational principle gives $0<\lambda_2(F)<1$.

At equality, necessarily $m\geq2$, and $z=-m(m-1)$, with $E/D=1$.
At every vertex of $R$ whose support contains $t$, the full Laplacian
action on $f$ is zero: its neighbors are in $R$ and have the same
trial value. But $f=-m(m-1)\ne0$ there, so $f$ is not an eigenvector
at one. Equality in the Rayleigh minimum would force $f$ to be such
an eigenvector. Hence $\lambda_2(F)<1$ also at the boundary.

In both cases, the complement identity reflects this positive
eigenvalue to $N-\lambda_2(F)$, where $N=A+m+(m+1)B$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:join} then shifts it by $mA-1$, giving
$V-\lambda_2(F)\in(V-1,V)$.
\end{proof}

The exact product condition has a convenient reciprocal sufficient
condition, giving unbounded families with every exponent at least two.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:small-exponent-reciprocal}
Let $n$ have $t\geq3$ distinct prime factors, one exponent $m\geq2$
and other positive integer exponents $k_1,\ldots,k_{t-1}$.
Suppose
\[
 \sum_{i<t}\frac1{k_i}\leq\frac1{m^2}.
\]
Then $G_n$ is not Laplacian integral. In particular, every
$k_i\geq m^2(t-1)$ suffices.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Put $u_i=1/k_i$ and $s=\sum u_i<1$. Induction gives
$\prod(1-u_i)\geq1-s$, while $1+u_i<(1-u_i)^{-1}$. Therefore
\[
 \frac BA=\prod_{i<t}(1+u_i)-1-\frac1A
 <\frac1{1-s}-1\leq\frac1{m^2-1}.
\]
Thus $A>(m^2-1)B$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:small-exponent-rayleigh}
applies. The stated coordinate bound implies the reciprocal bound.
\end{proof}

For example, $(10,10,10,2)$ has $A=1000,B=330$ and satisfies
the strict condition. The boundary vector $(4,19,74,2)$ has
$A=5624,B=1875$ and $A=3B-1$; the non-eigenvector argument in the
proof supplies strictness. The criterion does not apply to
$(2,2,2,2)$, for which $A=8,B=18$. It is a sufficient obstruction,
and the higher-prime classification outside it remains open.

The general six-support reduction also gives an exact diagnostic for
fully distinct triples. For $1\leq a<b<c\leq7$, all thirty-five quintics
have a nonlinear irreducible factor over $\mathbb Q$. The quintics
at $(1,2,7)$ and $(2,3,5)$ have factor degrees $(1,4)$, and that at
$(2,4,6)$ has factor degrees $(2,3)$;
irreducibility of the entire quintic is therefore not a
uniform obstruction. Theorem~\ref{thm:unit} covers every triple beginning
with $1$ without a range restriction; the thirty-five-case calculation
itself remains finite evidence.

\subsection{Four prime factors and two exponents equal to two}

A repeated pair also produces a small invariant subspace in four
coordinates. This settles a family including balanced vectors outside
the preceding Rayleigh criterion.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:four-prime-pair-two}
For distinct primes $p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4$ and every $b,c\geq2$, the graph
$G_{p_1^2p_2^2p_3^bp_4^c}$ is not Laplacian integral.
Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:unit-any-prime-count}, this covers every
positive four-prime exponent vector with at least two entries equal to two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Remove the $4bc-1$ universal vertices and let $F=\overline H$.
For each $T\subseteq\{3,4\}$, assign a function the values $h(T)$
on support $\{1\}\cup T$, $-h(T)$ on $\{2\}\cup T$, and zero elsewhere.
The paired classes have equal weight, so the function has weighted sum
zero. Classes containing neither or both of coordinates $1,2$ see
equal opposite contributions. At support $\{1\}\cup T$, the complement
degree is $3\prod_{i\in\{3,4\}\setminus T}(k_i+1)-1$ and its
nonzero-valued neighbors are precisely the classes $\{2\}\cup T'$
with $T'\cap T=\varnothing$, of weight $2\prod_{i\in T'}k_i$.
Thus the four-dimensional space is invariant. In the order
$T=\varnothing,\{4\},\{3\},\{3,4\}$, its complement action is $K-I$,
where
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
3(b+1)(c+1)+2&2c&2b&2bc\\
2&3(b+1)&2b&0\\
2&2c&3(c+1)&0\\
2&0&0&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The matrix $\operatorname{diag}(1,c,b,bc)K$ is symmetric. If $\kappa$
is an eigenvalue of $K$, complement reflection and the zero-sum
lifting calculation in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:join}
give the graph eigenvalue $V+1-\kappa$, with $V=9(b+1)(c+1)-2$.

The leading principal minors of $K-I$ are
\begin{align*}
D_1={}&3bc+3b+3c+4,\\
D_2={}&9b^2c+9b^2+15bc+18b+2c+8,\\
D_3={}&15b^2c^2+33b^2c+6b^2+33bc^2+84bc\\
       &{}+28b+6c^2+28c+16,\\
D_4={}&2\bigl(5b^2c^2+21b^2c+6b^2+21bc^2+76bc\\
       &\hspace{1.5em}{}+28b+6c^2+28c+16\bigr).
\end{align*}
All coefficients are positive. The symmetric similar matrix has the
same leading minors, so Sylvester's criterion gives $\kappa_{\min}>1$.
The principal submatrix of $K-3I$ on coordinates $1,4$ has determinant
$-4bc$, hence a negative Rayleigh quotient in its symmetric similar
matrix. Thus $\kappa_{\min}<3$. Unless $\det(K-2I)=0$, this smallest
eigenvalue is noninteger and so is its graph lift.

Direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
P(b,c):=\det(K-2I)={}&-5b^2c^2+12b^2c-3b^2+12bc^2\\
                    &{}+48bc+8b-3c^2+8c+3.
\end{align*}
Order $b\leq c$. For $b=7+u,c=7+v$, with $u,v\geq0$,
\begin{align*}
-P(7+u,7+v)={}&5u^2v^2+58u^2v+164u^2+58uv^2+596uv\\
              &{}+1364u+164v^2+1364v+1600>0.
\end{align*}
For the remaining five values of $b$, the polynomial is
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
b&P(b,c)\\\hline
2&c^2+152c+7\\
3&4c(65-3c)\\
4&-35c^2+392c-13\\
5&-4(c-8)(17c-1)\\
6&-111c^2+728c-57
\end{array}
\]
The first two rows have no positive integer zero. The discriminants
of rows $4,6$ are $151844\in(389^2,390^2)$ and
$504676\in(710^2,711^2)$, so neither has a rational zero.
The only positive integer endpoint pair is therefore $(b,c)=(5,8)$,
up to exchange.

At this pair,
\[
\det(xI-K)=(x-2)(x^3-210x^2+7701x-53240).
\]
The cubic reduces to $x^3+11x^2+4$ modulo $17$. At the residues
$0,\ldots,16$ its values, in order, are
\[
\begin{gathered}
4,16,5,11,6,13,4,2,13,\\
9,13,14,1,14,8,6,14.
\end{gathered}
\]
None vanishes, so the cubic is irreducible over $\mathbb F_{17}$ and
over $\mathbb Q$. Its roots are real eigenvalues of $K$ and irrational;
their graph lifts settle the exceptional pair as well. The proof uses
the five symbolic rows and this $17$-residue certificate, with no
finite exponent base.
\end{proof}

In particular, $(2,2,2,2)$ is nonintegral, although it fails
Theorem~\ref{thm:small-exponent-rayleigh}'s sufficient condition.
The present argument uses an actual invariant subspace, whereas the
preceding three-collection compression supplies trial functions.

\subsection{Two exponents equal to three}

Tensor normalization supplies a general lower bound for the
repeated-pair restriction. At exponent three it leaves an endpoint
equation with no integer solutions.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:four-prime-pair-three}
For distinct primes $p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4$ and every $b,c\geq2$, the graph
$G_{p_1^3p_2^3p_3^bp_4^c}$ has a noninteger Laplacian eigenvalue in
$(V-2,V)$, different from $V-1$, where $V=16(b+1)(c+1)-2$.
Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:unit-any-prime-count}, this proves
nonintegrality for every positive four-prime vector with two entries
equal to three.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the general vector $(a,a,b,c)$, use the paired-support functions
of Theorem~\ref{thm:four-prime-pair-two}. Their weighted sum is zero.
The degree on $\{1\}\cup T$ is now
$(a+1)\prod_{i\in\{3,4\}\setminus T}(k_i+1)-1$, while the opposite
neighbor classes have weights $a\prod_{i\in T'}k_i$.
Thus the complement restriction is $K_a-I$, with
\[
 K_a=(a+1)E_b\otimes E_c+aF_b\otimes F_c,\qquad
 E_k=\begin{pmatrix}k+1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 F_k=\begin{pmatrix}1&k\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The symmetrizer is $\operatorname{diag}(1,c,b,bc)$, and an eigenvalue
$\kappa$ lifts to $V+1-\kappa$, where
$V=(a+1)^2(b+1)(c+1)-2$, by the zero-sum calculation above.

In the weighted symmetric basis, $F_k$ becomes
$S_k=\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt{k}\\\sqrt{k}&0\end{pmatrix}$.
Put $T_k=E_k^{-1/2}S_kE_k^{-1/2}$. Its characteristic polynomial
is $(x-1)(x+k/(k+1))$, so its eigenvalues are $1,-k/(k+1)$.
Every eigenvalue of $T_b\otimes T_c$ is strictly greater than $-1$.
With $E=E_b\otimes E_c$, the symmetric similar matrix of $K_a$ is
\[
 E^{1/2}\bigl((a+1)I+aT_b\otimes T_c\bigr)E^{1/2}.
\]
The bracket is strictly greater than $I$, so this matrix is strictly
greater than $E\geq I$. Hence $\kappa_{\min}(K_a)>1$ for all
$a,b,c\geq1$.

Set $a=3$ and write $K=K_3$. Direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
\det(K-3I)={}&-35b^2c^2+8b^2c-20b^2+8bc^2\\
             &{}+109bc+11b-20c^2+11c+4.
\end{align*}
For $b=2+u,c=2+v$, its negative is
\begin{align*}
35u^2v^2+132u^2v+144u^2+132uv^2+387uv\\
{}+315u+144v^2+315v+108>0.
\end{align*}
The symmetric similar matrix $K-3I$ therefore has a negative
eigenvalue, giving $1<\kappa_{\min}(K)<3$. It suffices to exclude two.

Write $P(b,c)=\det(K-2I)$. Its exact polynomial is
\begin{align*}
P(b,c)={}&-7b^2c^2+48b^2c-8b^2+48bc^2\\
         &{}+302bc+76b-8c^2+76c+40.
\end{align*}
Order $b\leq c$. For $b=17+u,c=17+v$, with $u,v\geq0$,
\begin{align*}
-P(17+u,17+v)={}&7u^2v^2+190u^2v+1215u^2+190uv^2+4526uv\\
                &{}+22228u+1215v^2+22228v+27721>0.
\end{align*}
For $2\leq b\leq16$, write $P=A_bc^2+B_bc+C_b$, where
\[
A_b=-7b^2+48b-8,\quad B_b=48b^2+302b+76,\quad C_b=-8b^2+76b+40.
\]
For $b=2,\ldots,6$, the $A_b$ values are $(60,73,72,57,28)$
and the $C_b$ values are $(160,196,216,220,208)$; also $B_b>0$.
These rows have no positive zero. Two further rows factor as
\[
P(10,c)=-12c(19c-658),\qquad P(14,c)=-4(3c-58)(59c-2).
\]
Their positive roots $658/19,58/3,2/59$ are not integers.
For the remaining eight rows, the discriminant $D_b=B_b^2-4A_bC_b$
lies strictly between the consecutive squares shown below:
\[
\begin{array}{r|r|r}
b&D_b&h_b\text{ with }h_b^2<D_b<(h_b+1)^2\\\hline
7&20640564&4543\\
8&30997264&5567\\
9&44692596&6685\\
11&84630100&9199\\
12&112262544&10595\\
13&146014164&12083\\
15&235204596&15336\\
16&292433040&17100
\end{array}
\]
Thus none of these quadratics has a rational zero. The complete
symbolic reduction proves $P(b,c)\ne0$ for all integers $b,c\geq2$,
with no exceptional pair. Therefore $\kappa_{\min}$ is noninteger
in $(1,3)$, and its graph lift $V+1-\kappa_{\min}$ has the stated
location. The fixed arithmetic consists of the eight discriminants
and two factorizations; no finite two-parameter exponent base is used.
\end{proof}
