\section{Remaining questions}

Theorem~\ref{thm:repeated} settles every triple with at least two equal
exponents, and Theorem~\ref{thm:unit} settles every triple with a unit
exponent. The cases with three pairwise distinct exponents all at least two,
and nonsquarefree
exponent vectors with more than three prime factors, require further
arguments. The results here therefore do not establish nonintegrality
for every integer with at least three distinct prime factors.

The remaining three-prime question is whether $G_{p^a q^b r^c}$ is
always nonintegral when $a,b,c\geq2$ are pairwise distinct. The
coordinate-swap decomposition used for repeated exponents is unavailable
there; the full six-support quotient provides an exact starting point.

The distinction between the two repeated-exponent blocks remains essential: an integral
antisymmetric block occurs on the boundary family, while an integral
symmetric cubic occurs at $(1,1,2)$. The completion theorem uses both
blocks, rather than asserting that either alone always obstructs integrality.
