\section{Remaining questions}

Theorem~\ref{thm:repeated} settles every triple with at least two equal
exponents, and Corollary~\ref{cor:minimum-seven} settles every triple with
minimum exponent at most seven. Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct-tail}
settles every ordered triple with $c\geq4a^2-2a$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-gaps} settles four further unequal-exponent
families, including every triple with maximum minus minimum at most three.
The remaining three-prime cases therefore lie within
$8\leq a<b<c<4a^2-2a$, outside those families and the triples meeting
Proposition~\ref{prop:low-spectrum} or
Corollary~\ref{cor:balanced-interval}. In particular, they satisfy
 $b\geq16$ by the complete endpoint certificate of
Corollary~\ref{cor:middle-fifteen}, as well as
$(a-2)(a+b+c-2)\leq2bc$ and $a<3(c-a)+8$.
These cases and nonsquarefree
exponent vectors with more than three prime factors require further
arguments. The results here therefore do not establish nonintegrality
for every integer with at least three distinct prime factors.
Theorems~\ref{thm:common-divisor} and~\ref{thm:congruence} additionally
restrict the remaining three-prime triples to gcd one or two, at least
one even exponent, and residues outside the proved congruence classes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:modulo-three-endpoints} also excludes the residue
patterns $(2,2,2)$ and all permutations of $(1,2,2)$ modulo three.
Corollary~\ref{cor:common-valuation} also requires unequal $2$-adic
valuations. In the remaining all-even region at least one exponent is
congruent to two modulo four and at least one is divisible by four.
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-parity-congruence} also excludes six mixed-parity
residue classes modulo eight, without a size bound.

The remaining three-prime question is whether $G_{p^a q^b r^c}$ is
always nonintegral in this remaining region. For each fixed minimum
exponent $a$, the cutoff leaves a finite set of pairs; it does not
bound $a$ itself. The
coordinate-swap decomposition used for repeated exponents is unavailable
there. The full six-support quotient and its diagonal-minus-rank-one
Schur complement provide exact tools for the remaining argument.
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-low-root} now places a positive quotient
eigenvalue in $(0,3)$ throughout the remaining region. A possible
integral graph must therefore satisfy $h_C(1)h_C(2)=0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-two-bound} confines the second surface to
$c<7a-16+40/(a+2)$. For all-even triples one is excluded, so the
remaining gcd-two even region has this linear upper bound as well.
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-divisors} gives finite divisor candidates
for either surface at a fixed pair $(a,b)$.
Excluding integral triples on these two endpoint-zero surfaces subject
to the remaining arithmetic restrictions, or
finding another noninteger quotient root there, is the next focused
question. The necessary endpoint-zero condition is not itself an
integrality characterization.

The distinction between the two repeated-exponent blocks remains essential: an integral
antisymmetric block occurs on the boundary family, while an integral
symmetric cubic occurs at $(1,1,2)$. The completion theorem uses both
blocks, rather than asserting that either alone always obstructs integrality.
