\section{Remaining questions}

The squarefree composite classification and every repeated-exponent triple
are settled. For three distinct primes, the remaining ordered cases have
\[
 8\leq a<b<c<4a^2-2a,\qquad b\geq16,\qquad \gcd(a,b,c)\in\{1,2\},
\]
unequal $2$-adic valuations and at least one even exponent. They lie outside
the bounded-gap families and satisfy
$(a-2)(a+b+c-2)\leq2bc$ and $a<3(c-a)+8$. The common-residue exclusions,
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-residue-classes} and the modulo-three classes in
Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-reduction} impose the additional stated restrictions.
The following questions remain open.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the fully distinct endpoint-one surface be excluded, or can
another quotient root be shown noninteger on it? Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-low-root}
reduces possible integer spectra to $h_C(1)h_C(2)=0$, but vanishing of
one endpoint is not an integrality characterization. On endpoint one,
Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-one-spectrum} places all four residual roots
above $a$ and their smallest below $\min\{c,a+b\}$. Their nonzero constant
gives the necessary divisor test in
Corollary~\ref{cor:endpoint-one-divisor-window}; whether the quartic splits
entirely into integer roots remains open. For fully distinct triples the
new sum restriction $b+c>2a^2-2a+1$ is strict.
\item Can the endpoint-two surface be excluded inside
$c<7a-16+40/(a+2)$, subject to the applicable arithmetic restrictions?
All-even triples and the mixed patterns $(1,3,2)$ and $(3,3,0)$ must lie
on this surface. For a fixed pair $(a,b)$ the endpoint divisor conditions
give finitely many candidates; the minimum $a$ remains unbounded.
\item Can the surviving $(1,3,2)$ arithmetic classes be resolved uniformly
at the polynomial level? Finite closure by further two-adic shifts is
not established. The endpoint-congruence limitation in Appendix C concerns
finite endpoint-value tests with the explicitly listed filters; it does
not exclude another-root or additional arithmetic obstructions.
\item Which nonsquarefree exponent vectors with more than three prime
factors give integral graphs? The present arguments do not supply the full
general-exponent Q3 characterization.
\end{enumerate}

The distinction between the repeated-exponent blocks remains useful for
these questions. The boundary family has an integral antisymmetric block,
while $(1,1,2)$ has an integral symmetric cubic and a nonintegral full
spectrum. The complete repeated-exponent proof uses both blocks.
