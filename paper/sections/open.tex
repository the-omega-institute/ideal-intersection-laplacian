\section{Remaining questions}

Theorem~\ref{thm:repeated} settles every triple with at least two equal
exponents, and Corollary~\ref{cor:minimum-seven} settles every triple with
minimum exponent at most seven. Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct-tail}
settles every ordered triple with $c\geq4a^2-2a$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-gaps} settles four further unequal-exponent
families, including every triple with maximum minus minimum at most three.
The remaining three-prime cases therefore lie within
$8\leq a<b<c<4a^2-2a$, outside those families and the triples meeting
Proposition~\ref{prop:low-spectrum}. These cases and nonsquarefree
exponent vectors with more than three prime factors require further
arguments. The results here therefore do not establish nonintegrality
for every integer with at least three distinct prime factors.

The remaining three-prime question is whether $G_{p^a q^b r^c}$ is
always nonintegral in this remaining region. For each fixed minimum
exponent $a$, the cutoff leaves a finite set of pairs; it does not
bound $a$ itself. The
coordinate-swap decomposition used for repeated exponents is unavailable
there. The full six-support quotient and its diagonal-minus-rank-one
Schur complement provide exact tools for the remaining argument.

The distinction between the two repeated-exponent blocks remains essential: an integral
antisymmetric block occurs on the boundary family, while an integral
symmetric cubic occurs at $(1,1,2)$. The completion theorem uses both
blocks, rather than asserting that either alone always obstructs integrality.
