\begin{abstract}
We study Laplacian integrality for the graph of nonzero proper ideals of
$\mathbb Z_n$, where distinct ideals are adjacent when their intersection
is nonzero. For squarefree composite $n$, the graph is Laplacian integral
exactly when $n$ has two prime factors. For three distinct primes we prove
nonintegrality for every exponent vector $(a,a,b)$ with $a,b\geq1$,
including $(1,1,k)$, and for every three-prime exponent vector with
minimum entry at most seven. For arbitrary ordered exponents
$2\leq a\leq b\leq c$, we also prove nonintegrality whenever
$c\geq4a^2-2a$.
An inertia criterion also settles every triple whose maximum minus minimum
is at most three and the family $(a,a+3,a+4)$ for all $a\geq1$.
The repeated-exponent proof combines arithmetic constraints on a
quadratic discriminant with a uniform consecutive-integer interval for a
symmetric cubic. Exact divisor reductions settle the few exceptional
factor levels. Complement-quotient intervals prove the families with
an exponent equal to one, two or three; the last case uses an exhaustive
integer endpoint certificate after the uniform cutoff.
An independent integer discriminant certificate exhausts the finite
regions left by the cutoff for minimum entries four through seven.
The inertia argument counts two low complement eigenvalues and excludes
integer endpoints by four root-free modular certificates.
The remaining unequal-exponent and nonsquarefree higher-prime cases stay open.
\end{abstract}
