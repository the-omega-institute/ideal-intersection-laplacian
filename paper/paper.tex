\begin{abstract}
We prove that the ideal intersection graph of
$\mathbb Z_{p^a q^b r^c}$ is Laplacian nonintegral for any three
distinct primes and every positive exponent triple. Adjacency means
nonzero intersection of distinct nonzero proper ideals. For squarefree
composite integers, the integral case is exactly two prime factors.

A six-support complement quotient reduces the three-prime problem to
a real symmetric matrix. Its uniform positive root below three forces
an integer spectrum onto one of two endpoint surfaces. On endpoint two,
a written large-minimum argument supplies another root in $(4,5)$;
the remaining geometric domain has a complete $8\,658$-triple certificate.
On endpoint one, an exponent-sum bound and written small-gap brackets
reduce the final obstruction to the largest quotient root. For real
$3\leq a\leq b\leq c$, $b-a\geq3$ and $c\geq a^2-a$, we prove
\[
 bc+a+b+c<\lambda_{\max}<bc+a+b+c+1.
\]
Complete positive polynomial identities and a Schur complement establish
this unit interval throughout its unbounded domain. It closes the
remaining integer spectra without enumerating the endpoint-one exponent
points or solving the residual quartic's integer-root equation.

The global classification combines these written arguments with the
established repeated-exponent and small-minimum results, including a
complete $27\,562$-triple minimum-through-seven certificate. We retain
further spectral gaps, arithmetic obstructions and local equality-curve
splitting results. Exact verification uses integer and rational arithmetic;
no Lean formalization is claimed.
For any number of prime factors at least three, a unit exponent
gives a noninteger eigenvalue in $(V-1,V)$ by an explicit complement
Rayleigh quotient below one, without a finite base.
For a distinguished exponent $m\geq2$, the same conclusion holds
whenever $A\geq(m^2-1)B-(m-1)$, including equality, where $A$ is the
product of the other exponents and $B=\prod_{i<t}(k_i+1)-1-A$.
In particular, every other exponent at least $m^2(t-1)$ suffices.
For four prime factors, a four-dimensional antisymmetric subspace
also settles every positive exponent vector with two entries equal to two.
Its smallest restricted eigenvalue lies in $(1,3)$; the only possible
integer endpoint pair has an irreducible residual cubic modulo $17$.
The analogous family with two exponents equal to three is nonintegral
as well: tensor positivity and a complete endpoint reduction exclude
the sole possible integer without an exceptional exponent pair.
The nonsquarefree higher-prime classification outside these families
remains open.
\end{abstract}
